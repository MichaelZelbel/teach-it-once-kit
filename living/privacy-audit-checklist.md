# The Five-Minute Privacy Audit (Chapter 23)

Once a quarter, in the same sitting as the memory spring-clean
(Chapter 10) and the register walk (Chapter 20). Open four doors,
fix what you find, done.

## The four doors

1. **Memory summary.** **Settings** then **Personalization**. Read the
   summary the way your partner would read it. Correct what is wrong,
   remove what should not be there.
2. **Your about-you page.** Same screen. Still true? Still nothing you
   would mind a stranger-with-your-password reading?
3. **Connected apps.** The settings section listing connected apps
   (called **Plugins** on the author's account as of this writing).
   Every connection must still earn its access. Disconnect what you
   stopped using. Red lines (Chapter 21) govern what remains.
4. **Shared links.** **Settings** then **Data controls**, **Shared
   links**, **Manage**. Delete every link you do not actively want
   alive; anyone holding one may still open it.

## The sorting rule (pin this)

**Give it your patterns, not your keys.**

- Patterns (in): who matters, projects, preferences, plans, voice.
- Keys (never): passwords, PINs, full card numbers, recovery codes,
  anything that IS access rather than information.
- Other people's secrets: the face test. Only what you would say with
  that person reading over your shoulder.

## The three lanes

- **Normal chat:** everyday life. It feeds your history and memory;
  that is the point.
- **Temporary chat** (**Turn on temporary chat**, top right): sensitive
  one-offs (health worries, salary talk, surprises). On-screen promise:
  no history, no training, no new memories; a copy may be kept up to
  30 days for safety. Note: your about-you page still applies, so it is
  private, not anonymous.
- **Signed out:** the rare question that should not know your name.

## One switch worth knowing

**Settings** then **Data controls**: **Improve the model for
everyone**. Off means your conversations stay out of model training.
